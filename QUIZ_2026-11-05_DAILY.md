### Q2 - conductor versus conduction

Original: `In the experiment, copper served as a good ______ of heat, carrying energy from the hot end to the cool end.`

Learner: A. `conduction`. Correct: D. `conductor`.

Copper is the material carrying heat, so it is a conductor. Conduction is
the process of heat transfer. Both choices are nouns: the material/process
meaning distinction decides, rather than only a noun-versus-adjective rule.
Memory: `Copper is a good conductor of heat; heat moves through it by conduction.`

### Q7 - management correct but uncertain; manager count-noun reasoning

Original: `The team's problems came from poor ______: tasks were not assigned clearly and deadlines were never tracked.`

Learner: A. `management` with ?, also considered B. `manager` possible.
Correct: A. `management`. Award the point; record uncertainty separately.

Reasoning: the learner correctly recognizes two plausible causes in Vietnamese:
poor management or a poor manager. In this exact English sentence, management
is the uncountable organizing-process noun, so from poor management is valid.
Manager is a singular count noun and needs a determiner in this position:
from a poor manager / from the poor manager. From poor manager is invalid.
Poor managers would also be grammatical, but that is not the singular option.
The follow-up clauses describe failures of organizing tasks and deadlines.

Memory: `The problems came from poor management.`
Alternative corrected sentence: `The problems came from a poor manager.`
