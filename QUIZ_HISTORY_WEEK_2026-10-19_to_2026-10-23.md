### Q1 - production versus produce

Original: `The factory paused ______ for two days while workers repaired the assembly line.`

Learner: D. `produce?`. Correct: C. `production`.

Reasoning: The learner correctly understood that manufacturing was paused.
The remaining error is word form: paused takes the noun production as its
object here. Produce is the manufacture/create verb; bare produce does not
fit after paused in this sentence. Assembly line means a manufacturing or
assembly line, which the workers repaired during the pause.
Memory: `The factory paused production; the workers stopped producing goods.`

### Q7 - geochemical versus geochemistry

Original: `The study's findings were mainly ______, dealing with the chemical composition of rocks and soil.`

Learner: A. `geochemistry`. Correct: B. `geochemical`.

Why: The sentence describes the findings with adjective geochemical,
meaning related to Earth's chemistry. Geochemistry is the field noun,
not the description needed after findings were mainly. This repeats the
2026-10-16 field-noun versus adjective error in a clearer predicate context.
Memory: `The study focuses on geochemistry; its findings are geochemical.`

### Q8 - geographer versus geologist

Original: `The city hired a ______ to study how people and services are distributed across its neighborhoods.`

Learner: A. `geologist`. Correct: D. `geographer`.

Why: Distribution of people and services across places belongs to geography,
so the specialist is a geographer. A geologist studies rocks, Earth's
structure, and geological processes. Both options name people; the error
is specialization/meaning rather than part of speech.
Memory: `A geographer maps where people live; a geologist examines rock layers.`

### Q9 - boots versus outfits

Original: `Before walking through the deep mud, he put on knee-high rubber ______.`

Learner: D. `outfits?`. Correct: B. `boots` (canonical lemma boot).

Reasoning: The learner correctly read knee-high as reaching the knees.
Rubber means cao su; combined with knee-high and walking through mud, it
describes boots, or ung, rather than outfits, whole sets of clothes.
Put on can mean wear/pull on clothing or footwear. The full phrase means
he put on rubber boots reaching his knees before walking through deep mud.
Memory: `He put on knee-high rubber boots to walk through the mud.`

### Q8 - graduate versus gradual

Original: `The company hired a recent university ______ who had just completed her degree in engineering.`

Learner: A. `gradual`. Correct: C. `graduate`.

Why: The company hires a person, and the clause who had completed her

over time; it is an adjective, not the person needed here. Graduation names
the completion/event, while gradually is an adverb; neither is a person.
Memory: `The company hired a recent graduate; her responsibilities increased gradually.`
