### Q2 - produce versus product

Original: `The new factory will ______ electric bicycles for the local market.`

Learner: C. `product`. Correct: B. `produce`.

Will takes a base-form verb here: produce means make goods. Product is the
noun naming the item made; it cannot fill this verb slot. This repeats the
production-family role contrast and stays P1.
Memory: `The factory will produce bicycles. Bicycles are its products.`

### Q8 - particularly versus particular

Original: `The new search tool is ______ helpful when a folder contains thousands of files.`

Learner: A. `particular`. Correct: D. `particularly`.

The blank modifies adjective helpful, so it needs the adverb particularly,
meaning especially or more than usual. Particular is an adjective describing
a specific thing: a particular tool. The source's narrow Vietnamese gloss
does not make particular and particularly interchangeable.
Memory: `This particular tool is particularly helpful.`

### Q11 - written permission versus a written permit

Original: `You must get written ______ from the owner before using the room for an event.`

Learner: A. `permit`, marked uncertain. Reasoning: written permit means
văn bản cho phép. Correct: B. `permission`.

Reasoning is semantically plausible: written permit is a valid noun phrase
for a permission document. In this exact sentence permit is a singular count
noun and needs a determiner: get a written permit. The unchanged sentence
has no a/the/possessive, so get written permit is not grammatical.
Permission means approval and is uncountable in this sense: get written
permission is grammatical. Do not teach that written permit itself is invalid.
Memory: `Get written permission from the owner.` / `Get a written permit.`
