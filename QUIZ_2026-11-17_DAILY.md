### Q3 - `produce` versus `product`

Original: `The new solar panels can ______ enough electricity to power the small office during the day.`

Learner: B. `product`. Correct: A. `produce`.

After the modal verb `can`, the blank requires a base-form verb. `Produce` is
the verb meaning make or create. `Product` is a noun naming a thing that is
made, so `can product` is not grammatical here.

Memory: `Solar panels produce electricity; electricity is their product.`

### Q9 - `chess` versus `bike`

Original: `In ______, each player moves pieces on a checkered board and tries to trap the other player's king.`

Learner: D. `bike`. Correct: B. `chess`.

Pieces, a checkered board, and a king describe the board game `chess`. In the
active Tactics entry, `bike` is a verb meaning ride a bicycle, so it cannot name
this game.

Memory: `They play chess on a checkered board; they bike along a road.`

### Q11 - `logical` versus `illogical`

Original: `Her troubleshooting plan was ______: test the simplest possible cause before replacing expensive parts.`

Learner: D. `illogical`. Correct: A. `logical`.

Testing a simple cause before replacing expensive parts follows a sensible,
ordered line of reasoning, so the plan is `logical`. `Illogical` means that the
reasoning is inconsistent or does not make sense.

Memory: `Testing simple causes first is a logical troubleshooting plan.`
