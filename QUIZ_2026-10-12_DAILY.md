### Q5 - diction versus dictation

Original: `The podcast host worked on her ______ so listeners could distinguish every consonant.`

Learner: B. `dictation`. Correct: C. `diction`.

Why: `Diction` is the clarity, choice, and pronunciation of words. `Dictation`
is an exercise or process in which spoken words are written down.

Memory: `The host improved her diction; the students practiced dictation.`

### Q6 - conductive versus conduction

Original: `The engineer chose rubber for the handle because it is less electrically ______ than metal.`

Learner: A. `conduction`. Correct: B. `conductive`.

Why: After `is less electrically`, the sentence needs the adjective
`conductive`. `Conduction` is the noun for the process of carrying heat or
electricity.

Memory: `Copper is conductive, so electrical conduction occurs easily.`

### Q9 - flex versus flexor

Original: `The trainer asked her to ______ her ankle slowly to test its range of motion.`

Learner: B. `flexor`. Correct: C. `flex`.

Why: The structure `asked her to ...` requires a base-form verb, so `flex`
means bend the ankle. A `flexor` is a muscle that bends a joint.

Memory: `Flex your ankle; the flexor helps bend the joint.`

### Q12 - semiofficial versus semi-permanent

Original: `The agency shared a ______ notice with staff: management had approved it, but the minister had not signed the final version.`

Learner: A. `semi-permanent`. Correct: B. `semiofficial`.

Why: The clue is approval status: the notice is partly, but not fully,
official. `Semi-permanent` describes duration: long-lasting but not permanent.

Memory: `A semiofficial notice lacks final approval; semi-permanent ink
eventually fades.`
