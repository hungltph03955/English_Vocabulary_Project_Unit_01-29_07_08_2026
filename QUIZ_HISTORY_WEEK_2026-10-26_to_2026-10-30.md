### Q1 - conductive versus conduction

Original: `The engineer selected copper because it is highly ______ and allows electricity to pass through easily.`

Learner: B. `conduction`. Correct: C. `conductive`.

Why: The blank describes copper, so the needed word is the adjective
conductive, meaning able to carry electricity. Conduction names the process
of transferring heat/electricity. In this sentence, highly modifies the
adjective describing the material; the process noun cannot fill that role.
Memory: `Copper is conductive; conduction is the process.`

### Q5 - graduate versus graduation

Original: `As a recent university ______, she was applying for her first full-time job.`

Learner: B. `graduation`. Correct: C. `graduate`.

Why: A graduate is the person who finished university; graduation is the
completion or ceremony. The person applying for a job is a graduate.
Both choices are nouns here, so this is a person-versus-event meaning
contrast. The earlier graduate/gradual error involved an adjective;
do not reduce all graduate-family errors to the same POS explanation.
Memory: `After graduation, the graduate applied for her first job.`
